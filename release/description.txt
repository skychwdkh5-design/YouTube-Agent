The Great Salt Lake in Utah is the largest saline lake in the Western Hemisphere. In July 2022 it measured 4,190.2 feet - more than 20 feet below its 1986 record high. Nearly four decades of Landsat satellite images show where that water used to be.

In this video:
- real Landsat scenes from the U.S. Geological Survey, 1985 to 2022, shown with one color curve so the years can be compared
- why the lake fell: water diverted upstream for homes and farms, and a changing climate
- the 1959 railroad causeway that split the lake into a saltier north and a fresher south
- the 1987-1989 pumping project, when the lake was too high
- dust from the exposed lake bed, and what USGS does and does not conclude
- whether the record snow of 2022-2023 fixed the problem
