# geo-research

The brief is where the video is won. The script only performs what the research found.

## Before you start

1. Read `channel/research.md` - the source hierarchy and the ledger format. It is not optional.
2. Read `channel/editorial.md` - especially disputed territories and the words we treat as claims.
3. Create the working folder `channel/videos/<slug>/` from `channel/templates/` if it does not exist.
   The slug is lowercase-hyphenated: `northwest-angle`, `baarle-enclaves`.

## What to produce

**`brief.md`** (from the template):

- **The question** - the one thing the viewer will wonder in the first 15 seconds.
- **The answer** - in two sentences. If it takes more, the video is two videos.
- **Why it is surprising** - which assumption in the viewer's head it breaks.
- **The story spine** - cause, complication, consequence, today. Dates in order.
- **Three to five "wait, what?" facts** that can carry beats in the middle.
- **The U.S. bridge** - for a non-U.S. topic, the comparison that makes it land for an American
  ("an area about the size of Connecticut", "a border longer than the drive from Boston to Miami").
  The comparison is a claim and goes in the ledger.
- **Map frames** - the 3-6 frames the story needs (locator, zoom, overlay, before/after).
- **Open questions** - what the sources disagree on or do not answer.

**`claims.md`** (from the template): every population, area, distance, date, border fact, ranking,
statistic, superlative and "only/first/last" goes in as its own row with status UNVERIFIED and the
best source you found. geo-factcheck then verifies them.

## How to research

- **Start wherever, but end at a primary source.** Wikipedia and forums are fine for finding leads;
  follow the citation to the census table, statute, treaty, survey, or official agency page. The
  ledger records the primary, not the lead.
- **Record what the source actually says**, including its year and its definition (land area vs total
  area, city proper vs metro, census vs estimate). Most geography errors are definition errors.
- **Look for the counter-claim.** Search for the opposite of your most surprising fact. If it exists,
  it goes in Open questions and the claim's wording softens.
- **Disputed places**: record each side's position and who administers the territory today, with
  sources for both. Never pick a side in the brief.
- **Never fill a gap with a plausible number.** A missing figure is written as `[NEEDED: ...]`.

## What to hand back

The brief, the draft ledger, and a short list of the claims you are least sure of - those go to
geo-factcheck first.

## The gate

Nothing here publishes. This skill writes and you publish. Every output ends in a block the user
copies, and the last line of every run is the question: **ship it, or change it?**
