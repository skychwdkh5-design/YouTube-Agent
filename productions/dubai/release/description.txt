In November 2000, a NASA satellite looked down at a patch of the Persian Gulf off Dubai: open sea. In November 2003, the same place held a palm tree, fully formed. Three years apart.

This film follows NASA Earth Observatory's dated satellite images (ASTER on the Terra satellite, 2000-2011) and an astronaut photograph taken from the International Space Station in April 2022 to show how Dubai's palm-shaped islands were built, and what happened to the projects that came next.

What NASA describes, and what the images show:
- Sand dredged from the floor of the Gulf, placed where the island would be, protected by rock breakwaters
- GPS-guided placement of the sand and compaction with a machine called a vibroflot (NASA's description of the method)
- Palm Jumeirah in February 2002, October 2002 and November 2003
- Four projects in the 2006 image: Palm Jebel Ali, Palm Jumeirah, The World and Palm Deira
- The 2007-2008 global recession, which NASA says delayed these projects
- The 2022 astronaut photograph and what NASA's caption says about development

We stay inside the evidence. Every image carries its source, instrument and year, and we have no dated source for what happened after 2022, so the film does not guess.
