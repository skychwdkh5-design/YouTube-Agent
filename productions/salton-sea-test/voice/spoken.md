One window over the southern shore of California's Salton Sea. In October 2022, it holds 42,022 water-like pixels.

In October 2026, 33,784.

That is about 20 percent fewer.

According to a UC Riverside-led task force, the Congressional Research Service and the Water Education Foundation, the lake is shrinking mainly because less Colorado River water reaches it since the 2003 water transfers.

We could not open the full reports from our research setup.

We used four free Sentinel-2 scenes, all from the same orbit, taken in October of 2022, 2024, 2025 and 2026.

Same orbit means the same angle and time of day, so the shoreline lines up from year to year.

In that window, the water-like pixel count goes from 42,022 in 2022 to 38,436 in 2024, 37,550 in 2025 and 33,784 in 2026.

That is a steady decline, about 20 percent over four years.

We cannot say why the count fell from the images alone.

We did not measure the whole lake, only one window.

Water color, algae and the lake level on the day of each pass can change the count, and we cannot separate them here.

The satellites keep passing, so this picture will keep updating.
