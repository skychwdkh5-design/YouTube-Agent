# Salton Sea test episode - deliberately short: the verified facts are thin

## hook: The first fifteen seconds
One window over the southern shore of California's Salton Sea. In October 2022, it holds 42,022 water-like pixels. {C5}
In October 2026, 33,784. {C5}
That is about 20 percent fewer. {C5}

## plan: What has been said
According to a UC Riverside-led task force, the Congressional Research Service and the Water Education Foundation, the lake is shrinking mainly because less Colorado River water reaches it since the 2003 water transfers. {C1}
We could not open the full reports from our research setup. {L}

## method: How we looked
We used four free Sentinel-2 scenes, all from the same orbit, taken in October of 2022, 2024, 2025 and 2026. {C6}
Same orbit means the same angle and time of day, so the shoreline lines up from year to year.

## shows: What the satellites show
In that window, the water-like pixel count goes from 42,022 in 2022 to 38,436 in 2024, 37,550 in 2025 and 33,784 in 2026. {C5}
That is a steady decline, about 20 percent over four years. {C5}

## limits: What we cannot say
We cannot say why the count fell from the images alone. {L}
We did not measure the whole lake, only one window. {L}
Water color, algae and the lake level on the day of each pass can change the count, and we cannot separate them here. {L}

## close: What to watch
The satellites keep passing, so this picture will keep updating.
