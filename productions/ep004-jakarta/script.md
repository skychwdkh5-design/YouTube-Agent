# EP004 - Jakarta Is Sinking: Satellites Reveal Why. DRAFT v1 for the 2.5D REVIEW (NOT LOCKED until the owner approves the text). Every number maps to a used claim in story/claims.json via the claims= hint.
[VIS: claims=C01,C02 label="SENTINEL-1 RADAR, 2017-2018, LINE OF SIGHT"]
Every red patch on this map is ground that is sinking. In places, almost five inches in a single year. That is what radar satellites measured over Jakarta in 2017 and 2018.
[VIS: claims=C06]
And what they show is not one city sinking. It is several bowls, falling at different speeds.
[VIS: label="THREE QUESTIONS"]
So why is the ground falling? Why has one of those bowls slowed down? And why can't the same satellites see part of the coast?
[VIS: claims=C11 label="JAKARTA, JAVA, INDONESIA"]
Jakarta sits on the north coast of the island of Java, in Indonesia, on low, flat land beside the sea. By some estimates, as much as 40 percent of the city is already below sea level.
[VIS: claims=C02 label="HOW RADAR MEASURES - ILLUSTRATION"]
Here is how a satellite can feel the ground move from hundreds of miles up. It sends pulses of microwaves down and listens for the echo. Fly over the same spot again, and the echo comes back slightly shifted. That shift tells you the distance changed by a few millimeters.
[VIS: claims=C02 label="LINE OF SIGHT, NOT STRAIGHT DOWN"]
But notice what the satellite really measures: the change in distance along its line of sight, not straight down. That is why the map is labeled that way.
[VIS: claims=C03 label="WHAT IS UNDER THE CITY - ILLUSTRATION"]
So what is pulling the ground down? The European Space Agency points largely to groundwater being pumped out from underneath the city. Pump water out of the sand layers, and the pressure that held the ground up drops. Clay layers above and below can then squeeze together, and the surface sinks.
[VIS: claims=C32 label="SPRINGS BACK vs STAYS SQUEEZED"]
Researchers separate two things: ground that springs back a little, and ground that stays squeezed. This animation is an illustration, not a measurement.
[VIS: claims=C05,C06 derived="30" label="HOTSPOTS 2016-2024, LINE OF SIGHT"]
One analysis of radar data from 2016 to 2024 found three hotspots: one in the west, one on the northwest coast, and one in the northeast, near the harbor at Tanjung Priok. The fastest spot sank at about 30 millimeters a year, roughly an inch, along the line of sight.
[VIS: claims=C12 label="ARTIFICIAL ISLANDS - ONE SCIENTIST'S ESTIMATE"]
Out on the artificial islands off the coast, one scientist told reporters the ground could be dropping by about 80 millimeters a year.
[VIS: claims=C09 label="WHAT RADAR CANNOT SEE"]
Now the catch. To trust a pixel, the radar echo has to stay consistent from one pass to the next. In this study, pixels with a coherence below 0.6 were thrown out. And that filter removes pixels along the northern coast, the part of Jakarta closest to the sea.
[VIS: claims=C10 label="CHECK AGAINST GPS"]
Scientists check the radar against GPS. At one station, the radar rate came out 1.64 millimeters a year higher than the GPS vertical rate, with a typical error of 9.40 millimeters. And that station is not in the same spot as the radar pixel, so even this check has limits.
[VIS: claims=C07,C08 label="THE TWIST"]
Now the twist. The western hotspot started sinking fast in 2016, and then slowed down significantly. The northwest and northeast hotspots did not. They kept sinking in a straight line.
[VIS: claims=C27,C29,C31 label="2026 STUDY - ABSTRACT ONLY"]
A 2026 study, which we have only read as an abstract, also reports that some areas are slowing, a few even show localized uplift, and the coast keeps sinking.
[VIS: claims=C14,C15 label="WHY IT MATTERS - ILLUSTRATION"]
Why does it matter? Jakarta already floods. A flood in 2007 covered more than 70 percent of the city. Meanwhile global sea level was rising about 3.3 millimeters a year, according to 2021 reporting. In the worst spots, the ground drops much faster than that.
[VIS: label="WHAT SATELLITES PROVE"]
So what do satellites prove? That parts of Jakarta are sinking, at different speeds, and that some of that sinking is slowing. What can they not prove? What happens where the radar is blind, and exactly what is driving each bowl.
[VIS: claims=C02 label="CHECK THE LABEL"]
So the next time you see a map of a sinking city, check the label. What was measured, when, and in which direction.
