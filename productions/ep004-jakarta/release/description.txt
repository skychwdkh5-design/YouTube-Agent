Jakarta is sinking - but what do satellites actually measure, and what can't they prove?

In a few spots in Jakarta, European Space Agency radar showed the ground sinking almost five inches in a year, in 2017 and 2018 - a peak at individual spots, not a citywide average, measured along the satellite's line of sight. In this video we follow the evidence: how radar measures ground motion, why Jakarta is sinking (groundwater pumping, the weight of buildings and roads, young river mud), why one sinking area has slowed down, and why the same satellites cannot see part of the coast.

Every number on screen is labeled: line of sight or vertical, what was measured, and when. Where a result comes from a preprint or only an abstract, the video says so.
