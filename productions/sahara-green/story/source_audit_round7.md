## The $2 trillion figure (G4): REJECTED as an attribution
It is not in Ornstein et al. 2009. The conclusion says only "Such multi-trillion-dollar projects provide lots of motivation for scientifically-creative entrepreneurs" (p.431), and "capital costs of the forests will exceed the revenues from any reasonable carbon tax" (p.431). It appeared in popular-science coverage. Our arithmetic from the paper's inputs: 500 mm/yr over 9.8×10⁸ ha is 4.9×10¹² m³/yr; at the paper's $0.53/m³ that is about $2.6 trillion a year. That is our calculation, assumes no induced rain and counts desalination only, and is not a figure the authors give. Removed from narration, storyboard and claims.

## Research-audit-only: cost figures that are in the paper (not narrated)
- Abstract: "Without any rain, to capture CO₂ produced from gasoline requires adding about $1 to the per-gallon pump-price to cover irrigation costs, using reverse osmosis (RO), desalinated, sea water." This is a desalination-and-irrigation operating cost for capturing the CO₂ from a gallon of gasoline (~2.3 kg of carbon), assuming no rain; it excludes capital costs.
- p.414: RO desalination "about $0.53 per cubic meter" (1.58 kWh/m³, half of the cost); p.415: 4.04 kWh/m³ with pumping to 450 m; $0.02 to $6.50 per m³ depending on the power source.
- p.416: "as little as $0.61" per gallon for desalination; about $0.87 with distribution pumping at $0.10/kWh; about $30 per barrel of crude (about $43 with pumping).
- p.431-433: capital costs exceed carbon-tax revenue; costs "may be lower—or higher".

## Evidence details
**G2** (Ornstein pp.410-412, 415, 427-428). Sahara irrigated at about 500 mm/yr (4.9×10¹² m³/yr) "might sequester from about 6 to 12 Gt C per year"; fossil-fuel emissions 7.2±0.3 Gt C/yr (2000-2005), about 8.8 with land use; a forest can accumulate carbon "for about a century, reaching a steady-state"; harvest and replanting could then supply about 7.2 Gt C/yr; "Such calculations combine a number of measurements with large error bars". In the GISS runs only GPP is computed; for the reduced forests, GPP × 0.45 gives about 8 Gt C/yr. Energy for desalination and pumping would offset 38% (coal), 22% (gas) or 1.8% (nuclear) of the benefit.

**G1b** (p.418, Fig. 3 p.419). Fixed-ocean run: rain increase "a minimum of about 700 mm/year, and well over 1,200 mm/year for about one half the Sahara"; coupled-ocean run: "over 1,000 mm/year for about one half". Rain falls April to November like the West African monsoon. Model: GISS ModelE, 4° × 5°, 20 years (10 spin-up, 10 analysed). Required irrigation in the western and southern Sahara reduced to the order of 300 mm/yr (fixed-ocean). Limits stated by the authors: coarse resolution; ModelE underestimates monsoon strength and Amazon rain; the coupled ocean has a cold Atlantic bias; "if confirmed with other GCMs" (abstract; p.431). Their own 1980s run, with poor irrigation code, gave only a weak increase (p.414).

**P24** (Pausata, pp.6221-6225). EC-Earth 3.1, 1.125° × 1.125°; four experiments at 6,000 years ago: orbital only (MHPMIP), greening + reduced dust (MHGS+RD), greening only, reduced dust only; Sahara set to evergreen shrub over 11-33°N, 15°W-35°E (albedo 0.3 to 0.15), dust reduced by up to 80%; 30 analysed years per run; synthetic cyclones by dynamical downscaling. Results: orbital forcing alone leaves Northern Hemisphere totals unchanged or slightly lower and raises Southern Hemisphere totals; adding greening and less dust strengthens the pattern, with a marked increase in the North Atlantic (Caribbean basin and southeastern US), the South China Sea, the Bay of Bengal and north-west Australia, and decreases offshore in the western Pacific and central tropical Atlantic. Greening acts worldwide; reduced dust acts mainly over the Atlantic, Caribbean and the US east coast. Limits: the model underestimates North Atlantic storms strongly (cold bias; only relative changes are used); vegetation and dust are "not meant to provide a faithful representation"; no complete paleostorm records exist to test it (one record from Vieques, Puerto Rico is consistent). The authors' only statement about the future is conditional (regreening could raise risk for the Caribbean, Gulf of Mexico and US east coast); it is not applied to planted forests.

## Why G1b and Kemena's +267 mm/yr must not be compared
Different models (GISS ModelE vs CESM-WACCM), different Sahara extents (a 16° × 50° swath vs an 18-33°N box), different irrigation schemes, and different metrics: ">1,000 mm/yr over about half of the irrigated area" vs "+267 mm/yr averaged over the Sahara". Kemena et al. themselves state that their estimate is "much smaller compared to earlier studies" including Ornstein. The narration says this once, and the storyboard keeps the two on separate scenes (24b, 25).
