## The question
Why does a chunk of land north of the 49th parallel, reachable by road year-round only through
Canada, belong to Minnesota?

## The answer (two sentences)
The 1783 peace treaty drew the border to the "most northwestern point" of Lake of the Woods and then
"due west" to the Mississippi, using a 1755 map that showed neither the lake nor the river's source
correctly - and the Mississippi turned out to start well south of the lake, so the line was
impossible [C2-C7]. When the 1818 Convention fixed it by dropping a line due south from that
northwestern point to the 49th parallel, and surveyors placed the point deep in Angle Inlet, the
land between that line, the parallel and the lake was left on the American side [C11, C14, C19].

## Why it is surprising
Viewers assume the U.S.-Canada border in the west "is" the 49th parallel. Here the border leaves the
parallel, runs north to a point fixed by 18th-century diplomacy, and the U.S. keeps a piece of land
whose only land neighbour is Canada.

## Fact / common simplification / myth

| "The Northwest Angle is a map error." | **SIMPLIFICATION** | The 1783 instruction relied on an inaccurate 1755 map, and the "due west to the Mississippi" line was impossible. But the Angle's shape comes from the 1818 fix (a line due south from the northwesternmost point) plus where surveyors located that point (1825). It was born from a map error and shaped by diplomacy. |
| "It was a surveying error." (MinnPost fact brief, 2023) | **SIMPLIFICATION / imprecise** | The surveys were the correction, not the error. The faulty knowledge was the 1755 map the negotiators used. |
| "Mitchell's map put the Mississippi's source north of Lake of the Woods." | **MYTH (as usually stated)** | The map does not show the source: the river runs up under an inset map. Negotiators assumed a due-west line would hit it [C5]. |
| "It's the only part of the Lower 48 north of the 49th parallel." | **NEEDS CHECK - possibly an over-statement** | Since 1925 the 49th-parallel boundary is straight segments between monuments, so tiny slivers may sit slightly off the parallel. ## The 49th parallel, made visual

1. **1818: the plains have no landmarks**, so diplomats pick a line of latitude - the 49th parallel -
   from Lake of the Woods west to the mountains [C11].
2. **But the treaty already had one fixed point in the east**: the lake's northwesternmost point,
   inherited from 1783 [C2].
3. **That point turns out to be north of the 49th** - roughly 27 miles north (computed from the
   treaty coordinates; claim C26).
4. **Rule: drop a line due south** from the point to the parallel, then run west [C11].
5. **The land caught east of that drop and north of the parallel**, on the lake's western shore, is the
   Angle [C19, C22].

Animation: draw the parallel across the plains → pin the point → drop the vertical line → shade the
trapped land → zoom to today's satellite view.

## Three story structures

**A. "The Impossible Line"** - chronological mystery. Estimated 10-11 min.
- Opening mystery: the bump on the map; the only year-round road runs through Canada.
- Escalation: 1783 - an instruction that sounds precise: northwestern point, then due west to the
  Mississippi.
- Midpoint reveal: the Mississippi starts about 2° of latitude south of the lake - the line can
  never reach it; the map never showed the river's source at all.
- Final payoff: the 1818 "due south" fix plus the 1825 survey point trap the land on the U.S. side;
  today's 149 residents live with remote border check-ins - changing again on Dec 1, 2026.
- Risk: the history middle (1794-1842) can sag - needs strong map animation.

**B. "Follow the Drive"** - modern-first, then zoom out. Estimated 9-11 min.
- Opening mystery: drive from Warroad - leave the U.S., about 40 miles of Manitoba, report at
  Jim's Corner, re-enter Minnesota.
- Escalation: why would any border do this? Zoom out to the 49th parallel - the Angle breaks it.
- Midpoint reveal: the 1755 map and the impossible "due west" instruction.
- Final payoff: the 1818 rule draws the chimney on screen; return to the road: in winter there
  is a way across the ice - and from December 2026 the check-in changes again.
- Risk: needs road visuals (maps + Landsat work; no licensed road footage yet).

**C. "Everything You've Heard Is Slightly Wrong"** - myth-buster. Estimated 8-9 min.
- Opening mystery: three things everyone says about the Angle - a map error, the only place north
  of the 49th, you must drive through Canada. All three are only half true.
- Escalation: each myth corrected with a primary source.
- Midpoint reveal: the real mechanism - the 1818 rule and the 1825 point.
- Final payoff: what is literally true, stated in one sentence.
- Risk: correcting myths up front gives answers before curiosity builds; fact-heavy; three claims
  still unverified.

**Recommendation: B, with A's midpoint.** It opens on the strongest verified-able anomaly (the road),
gives the map something to do from second one, and saves the 1755 map for the reveal. C's myth
corrections become short asides inside B.
