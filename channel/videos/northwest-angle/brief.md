## The question
Why does a chunk of land north of the 49th parallel, reachable by road year-round only through
Canada, belong to Minnesota?

## The answer (two sentences)
The 1783 peace treaty drew the border to the "most northwestern point" of Lake of the Woods and then
"due west" to the Mississippi, using a 1755 map that showed neither the lake nor the river's source
correctly - and the Mississippi turned out to start well south of the lake, so the line was
impossible [C2-C7]. When the 1818 Convention fixed it by dropping a line due south from that
northwestern point to the 49th parallel, and surveyors placed the point deep in Angle Inlet, the
land between that line, the parallel and the lake was left on the American side [C11, C14, C19].

Verification status of this answer: the treaty wording [C2, C11], the Mississippi lying far south
of the point [C7] and the 1925 point [C19] are verified. The map's role [C3-C6] and the 1825 Tiarks
survey [C14] are still UNVERIFIED - until they are, the script can say the 1783 negotiators assumed
a due-west line would reach the river (Jay Treaty, Art. 4 [C10], records that doubt by 1794), but
not name the Mitchell map or Tiarks as fact.

## Why it is surprising
Viewers assume the U.S.-Canada border in the west "is" the 49th parallel. Here the border leaves the
parallel, runs north to a point fixed by 18th-century diplomacy, and the U.S. keeps a piece of land
whose only land neighbour is Canada.

## The 49th parallel, made visual

1. **1818: the plains have no landmarks**, so diplomats pick a line of latitude - the 49th parallel -
   from Lake of the Woods west to the mountains [C11].
2. **But the treaty already had one fixed point in the east**: the lake's northwesternmost point,
   inherited from 1783 [C2].
3. **That point turns out to be north of the 49th** - about 27 miles north. The 1873 U.S. boundary
   report says the meridian runs "south twenty-seven miles to the 49th parallel"; the 1925 point
   computes to 26.6 miles [C26, VERIFIED].
4. **Rule: drop a line due south** from the point to the parallel, then run west [C11].
5. **The land caught east of that drop and north of the parallel**, on the lake's western shore, is the
   Angle [C11, C15, C19]. (Optional twist: by 1925 the surveyed lines crossed at five points and left
   two 2.5-acre pockets of U.S. water inside Canada - the 1925 treaty tidied that up [C43].)

Animation: draw the parallel across the plains → pin the point → drop the vertical line → shade the
trapped land → zoom to today's satellite view.
