# Editorial standards

What we will say, how we say it, and what we will not do. Every skill that writes words for the
channel follows this file. geo-factcheck enforces the factual part of it.

## 1. Accuracy is the product

- Every factual claim about **population, borders, area, dates, distances, rankings, statistics,
  superlatives, and "only / first / last / nobody"** is a ledger row in the video's `claims.md`
  and is verified against a reliable source before the script is final (`research.md`).
- The script uses the verified wording. If a claim is only true with a qualifier, the qualifier is
  in the narration, not just in our notes.
- Estimates are called estimates. Theories are attributed ("historians think", "according to the
  U.S. Geological Survey").
- A missing number is never filled with a plausible one. It is `[NEEDED]` until sourced, or the line
  is rewritten without it.

## 2. Words that are claims

These words make a sentence a factual claim and trigger the fact-check gate:
only, first, last, largest, smallest, longest, highest, most, least, nobody, no one, never, always,
every, record, number one, any figure, any year.

Prefer honest strength:

| Instead of | Write (when it is what the source supports) |
|---|---|
| "Nobody lives here" | "Almost nobody lives here" + the figure |
| "The most remote place on Earth" | "One of the most remote inhabited places on Earth" |
| "The only..." | "The only..." - only with two independent sources |
| "Scientists discovered..." | Name the institution, or do not say it |

## 3. Curiosity without clickbait

- The title and thumbnail may provoke; they may not lie. The video must deliver the exact thing the
  packaging promised, and the payoff is delivered explicitly.
- A question title must be answered in the video.
- No fake urgency, no fake controversy, no "they don't want you to know".
- Thumbnails show the real place or a faithful map of it. AI or edited thumbnails must not misstate
  where or what something is.

## 4. Disputed and sensitive territories

- Describe, do not decide. State who administers a territory today, who else claims it, and since
  when - each sourced.
- Use the name in common U.S. English usage, and mention the other names once where relevant.
- Maps of disputed areas show the line of control or administration with a clear "disputed" style
  (see `visual-style.md`), not one side's claim as settled fact.
- No topic is chosen to provoke a nationality or religious group. Ongoing wars and active conflicts
  are covered only for their geography, with extra care and dated sources - or not at all.
- Indigenous lands and names are described accurately and respectfully, with the current official
  status sourced.

## 5. Safety and privacy

- "Abandoned" and "isolated" places: never encourage trespassing or visiting dangerous sites; note
  private property or restricted access when relevant.
- No identifiable private individuals' homes or addresses. Satellite zooms stop at the level the
  story needs.

## 6. AI use and disclosure

- **AI narration** is a production choice. As of this writing, secondary summaries of YouTube's
  policy agree that AI voice-over in an original, scripted video does not by itself require the
  altered/synthetic-content disclosure; a voice cloned to sound like a real person would. We could
  not open YouTube's own help page from this environment, so **re-check the official
  "altered or synthetic content" help page before the first upload** and note the date here:
  _checked: TBD_.
- **AI visuals** must never pass as real imagery of a real place or event: no AI "satellite
  images", no AI "photos" of real towns, no AI "archival" footage. Stylized illustrations are fine.
- **Realistic AI visuals** (anything a viewer could mistake for real footage) turn on the
  altered/synthetic-content disclosure at upload. `assets.py` flags them.
- **Inauthentic / mass-produced content.** YouTube's monetization policies penalize repetitive,
  mass-produced, template-driven content, and faceless AI-voiced channels are the most exposed.
  Our protection is real editorial work: original research and ledger per video, original maps
  and animation, a distinct structure per topic, and scripts written for the topic - never one
  template with the names swapped. Re-read the current YouTube Partner Program policy before
  applying for monetization.

## 7. Corrections

- If a viewer shows we were wrong: verify, then pin a correction, fix the description, and log it in
  the video's `post.md` and in `research.md` under Lessons. No silent edits, no arguing.
- If we were right: one polite reply with the source.

## 8. Nothing publishes automatically

Every skill writes drafts. The creator reviews and publishes. No skill posts, uploads, replies or
schedules anything on YouTube.
