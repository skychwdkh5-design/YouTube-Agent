# Voice - the narrator

This is the voice profile every writing skill uses (yt-script, yt-shorts, yt-package, yt-seo,
yt-comment). The channel is faceless: the "voice" is an AI narrator reading a script, so it is
designed, not inferred from videos. Update it as the channel finds its sound.

## Persona

- A curious, sharp friend who just found something strange on a map and cannot wait to show you.
- Confident, not smug. Amused, not snarky. Never lectures.
- Talks TO the viewer ("you"), occasionally WITH them ("let's zoom in"). Uses "we" for the channel
  sparingly. Never "I" - there is no person to be "I", and the narrator never claims to have been
  somewhere, measured something, or met someone.

## Native American English

- **Spelling:** American (color, center, kilometer, traveled, defense, gray).
- **Units:** imperial first, because that is how the audience thinks: miles, square miles, feet,
  °F. Metric goes on screen in a smaller label when it helps, not in the narration.
- **Dates:** "July 4, 1776". Decades: "the 1840s". Centuries spoken: "the eighteen-hundreds" only
  when it reads naturally; otherwise "the 1800s".
- **Numbers in narration:** round and readable. "about 4,000 people", "nearly half", "roughly the
  size of Connecticut". Exact figures go on screen. Every number is a ledger claim either way.
- **Comparisons the audience owns:** U.S. states, cities, drives and landmarks. "About the size of
  Rhode Island", "a longer drive than New York to Chicago". These comparisons are claims too.
- **Contractions always:** it's, doesn't, you'd, there's, that's. Uncontracted sounds robotic.
- **Idiom, lightly:** "here's the thing", "it gets weirder", "on paper", "in practice". Never
  British idiom ("whilst", "fortnight", "brilliant" as praise, "quite" as "very").
- **Place names:** use the form U.S. viewers know (Ivory Coast in narration, Côte d'Ivoire on a
  label if needed; Turkey in narration, Türkiye explained once when relevant).

## Writing for an AI narrator

AI voices read exactly what is written. Write for the ear:

- **Short sentences.** 8-18 words, one idea each. A sentence that needs a breath in the middle gets
  split.
- **No parentheses, slashes, "e.g." or "i.e."** - they are read aloud badly. Write it out.
- **No abbreviations the voice may spell oddly** - write "United States" or "the U.S." (test both
  with the chosen voice), "square miles" not "sq mi", "percent" not "%" in the VO line.
- **Numbers as you want them heard** when the voice gets them wrong: "nineteen eighteen" vs "1918".
  Test each new number format once and record the result below.
- **Emphasis by structure, not by caps.** Put the surprising word at the end of the sentence.
- **Pauses:** a line break in the VO is a beat. An ellipsis is not used. Long pauses are added in
  the edit, not in the text.
- **Questions go up.** Use a real question when you want the voice to rise; do not rely on the voice
  to guess.

## Pace

- Planning default: **150 words per minute**. After the voice is chosen, time three finished
  scripts and replace this with the measured rate: _measured WPM: TBD_.
- First 15 seconds: shortest sentences of the video. Middle: vary length. Payoff: slow down.

## Words and moves we never use

- "In this video we will..." / "Before we get started..." / "Don't forget to like and subscribe"
  in the first minute.
- "Fun fact" (show it; do not label it), "mind-blowing", "insane", "you won't believe".
- "Literally" as emphasis, "basically", "actually" more than once per video.
- "Some people say..." without saying who.
- Textbook phrasing: "is located in", "is known for its", "the capital of X is".
- Any certainty stronger than the ledger allows.
